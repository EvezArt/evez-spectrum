## §2 THE RECEIPTS

RECEIPT 1 — GOOGLE, February 2026.
Public claim: "AI Ultra — our most advanced AI experience." ($249.99/month.)
The asterisk: users who connected their accounts to OpenClaw — a lawful open-source
agent framework — were suspended from the service they paid for. Google AI Developers
Forum thread documents: no warning, no explanation, automated appeal responses, one
user's new account restricted immediately. Reports raised — though Google did not
confirm — that restriction could spread across the shared Google identity (Gmail, Drive)
on the same account. Sources: SharTech analysis (2026-02-23), Implicator, Google AI
Developers Forum complaints.
The exposed qualifier: "most advanced AI experience*" (*at human speed only;
machine-speed use of what you paid for is grounds for suspension without warning.)

RECEIPT 2 — ANTHROPIC, February 2026.
Public claim: Claude subscriptions for building with AI.
The asterisk: OAuth tokens from consumer subscriptions banned from third-party tools.
Reporting (midens.com, 2026-02) states the terms had "technically prohibited this kind
of use for about two years" — the qualifier existed, was never enforced, was never
disclosed at point of sale — until February 2026, when enforcement arrived with account
lockouts, no grace period, minimal communication, and appeals that went nowhere.
The exposed qualifier: "build with Claude*" (*only inside our tools; the subscription
you bought for automation was never really for automation.)

RECEIPT 3 — X, February 23, 2026.
Public claim: "the API for the public conversation."
The asterisk: bots may only reply when the original author summons them by mention or

Source: Economic Times coverage of the developer API policy revision.
The exposed qualifier: "the public conversation*" (*agents speak only when spoken to.)

RECEIPT 4 — THE FTC'S OWN TEXT, July 1, 2026.
Public claim (the platforms'): "safe, accurate, aligned AI."
The FTC's proposed policy statement — "Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence
Systems" — says steering AI outputs while marketing accuracy is Section 5 deception,
that state-law compliance is NOT a safe harbor, and that Section 5 "secures consumers'
right to truth in the marketplace." The FTC wrote the exposure for us: models marketed
as accurate while suppressed, steered, or throttled behind the scenes are deceptions.
Source: ftc.gov, Proposed Policy Statement, 2026-07-01 (public PDF).

RECEIPT 6 — THE FINANCIAL WALL, 2026.
Agents cannot pass KYC, cannot hold accounts, cannot be merchants. Payment research

## §3 THE PATTERN (the point)
Read together, the receipts expose one structure:
1. The claims are sold to everyone. The qualifiers are enforced against the users
   who took the claims seriously. (Ultra subscribers. Automation builders. API developers.)
2. The enforcement is synchronized across competitors within weeks of each other.
   (February 2026: Anthropic, then Google, then X.)
3. The justification shifts between safety, capacity, and economics depending on the
   audience — but the FTC's own framework classifies the marketing-accuracy gap as
   deception regardless of which justification is used.
4. The one enforcement remedy actually used is account termination — self-help against
   the customer, never prosecution — because the criminal theory is dead law (Van Buren,
   2021; hiQ, 2019/2022) and they know it.
The asterisk regime is a revenue-defense perimeter wearing safety marketing. That is
the exposure. There is no asterisk on that sentence.
