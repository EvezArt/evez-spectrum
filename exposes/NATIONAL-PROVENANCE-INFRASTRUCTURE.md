## I. THE NATIONAL THREAT MAP (documented)

THREAT 1 — A FEDERAL OBLIGATION WITH NO INSTRUMENT
Executive Order 14319 (cited in the FTC's July 1, 2026 policy statement) declares agencies'
"obligation not to procure [AI] models that sacrifice truthfulness and accuracy to ideological
agendas." OMB guidance now requires procurement verification of model behavior.
The obligation exists. The instrument does not. No agency, no lab, no vendor ships a
tamper-evident method to prove what a model actually did — what was asked, answered,
steered, or suppressed. Procurement currently runs on vendor representations.

THREAT 2 — THE FIFTY-STATE PATCHWORK
The FTC's own statement frames the choice: "a burdensome national policy framework for AI —
not 50 discordant State ones." Colorado's revised AI Act (cited by name in the FTC PDF)
already mandates model alterations. The FTC says undisclosed steering is Section 5 deception.
The nation needs a unified, interstate truth-verification layer for AI — or every state
becomes its own model market with its own hidden deltas.

THREAT 3 — AGENT-MEDIATED ATTACKS ON CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE
The FBI warns of AI-generated fake monitoring tools spying on the industrial systems
running US power, water, and manufacturing plants. Security researchers call 2026's defining
shift "attacks built specifically for AI agents working in teams." AI companies warn of
cyberattack surges on hospitals, electrical systems, and water treatment. The Thai finance
ministry incident (Hacker News, 2026-07) showed the operational reality: unattended agents
with disabled approvals. The counter-forensics layer for AI-mediated attacks is evidence
chains — and evidence by accident (open logs) is not evidence by architecture.

THREAT 4 — THE OPEN-WEIGHTS COMPETITION
