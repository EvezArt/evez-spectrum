## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
EVEZ does not enter the market as a general AI consultant, quantum futurist, or custom-agent
developer. The position: technical assurance and decision-intelligence specialist for teams
whose AI, autonomy, simulation, or cryptographic claims must survive scrutiny. The buyer
buys a faster, more defensible answer to one of four expensive questions: Can this work?
Can we prove it? What fails in deployment? What should we fund or migrate first?

The price architecture ($5k-$10k diligence / $15k briefing / $3k-$5k-month advisory) is
commercially plausible as a productized solo-specialist offer, but public primary sources do
NOT establish these as market-standard rates. They are EVEZ target packaging, tested through
sales. The first sale is a bounded decision artifact: technical risk memo, evidence pack,
migration map, simulation-validation plan, or investment diligence brief.

## 2. TOP THREE SECTORS

### SECTOR 1 - AEROSPACE, ROBOTICS, AND AUTONOMY ASSURANCE
Why now: NASA frames autonomy V&V as a deployment and certification problem, with gaps from
AI/ML, planning, optimization, complex autonomous behavior [3].
Buyer pain: systems built faster than they can be explained - scenario coverage gaps, weak
requirement-to-test traceability, insufficient edge-case evidence, sim-to-reality
uncertainty, no answer to 'why should we trust this behavior?'
Budget owner: VP Eng/CTO (exploratory); autonomy/safety/verification lead (immediate); in
primes, systems engineering, software assurance, quality, certification, program management
control pieces. Startups: CTO/head of autonomy approves small external engagements directly.
Procurement: founder/CTO-sponsored paid assessment, then SOW tied to program milestone.
Primes require approved-vendor onboarding and longer cycles; startups buy fixed-scope direct.
Entry offer: Autonomy Evidence & Simulation Diligence - $7.5k-$10k target. Deliver: system
map; risk register; scenario taxonomy; evidence/telemetry schema; simulation and replay
plan; 10-20 prioritized failure modes; executive decision memo. EXCLUDES certification sign-off.
Expansion: $3k-$5k/month advisory (test strategy, architecture reviews, incident analysis,
evidence-pack maintenance, briefings) with fixed monthly capacity and named deliverables.
Why EVEZ fits: evez-agentnet combines named agents, orchestration, evidence, lineage, tests,
world simulation, tooling; evez-spine provides the provenance pattern. Demonstration assets
for traceability and replay - not certification evidence. [6][7]

### SECTOR 2 - POST-QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY MIGRATION AND CRYPTO-AGILITY
Why now: NIST says migrate now; inventory/discovery, prioritization, interoperability, and
replacement across hardware/software/services are the workstreams [1][2].
Buyer pain: unknown locations of public-key crypto, vendor/device dependencies, certificate
and protocol changes, systems that cannot tolerate larger keys/signatures or hybrid modes.
An inventory and dependency problem before an algorithm-selection problem.
Budget owner: CISO, security architecture, platform engineering, PKI/identity, compliance/risk;
product security and embedded engineering co-buy for device fleets.
Procurement: low-risk discovery assessment first (architecture diagrams, SBOMs, dependency
manifests, TLS/certificate inventories, code scans, interviews); follow-on maps prioritized
migrations and runs interoperability tests in a controlled environment [2].
Entry offer: PQC Exposure & Migration Diligence - $5k-$10k target. Deliver: crypto asset
inventory template; algorithm/protocol map; harvest-now-decrypt-later exposure triage;
vendor/dependency questions; migration sequencing; 90-day action backlog. No claims of
complete enterprise inventory unless scope covers the systems.
Expansion: $3k-$5k/month advisory (crypto-agility design reviews, vendor evaluation,
migration governance, interop-test planning); $15k briefing for security/platform/product
teams when alignment is the need.
Why EVEZ fits: evez-spine demonstrates tamper-evident capture, lineage, verification;
evez-cipher only after direct inspection confirms scope. Message: migration intelligence and
evidence. NEVER 'EVEZ replaces a crypto library' or 'EVEZ certifies PQC.'

### SECTOR 3 - SCIENTIFIC ML, PHYSICS SIMULATION, ENGINEERING DIGITAL TWINS
Why now: PhysicsNeMo positioning shows the market direction (physics-informed surrogates,
neural operators, GNNs, generative models, digital twins) [4]. The gap is not model creation;
it is fidelity choice, surrogate validation, uncertainty management, and integration into an
engineering decision process.
Buyer pain: expensive simulation cycles, scarce experts, inconsistent data, unclear model
validity outside training regimes, pressure to turn demos into repeatable workflows. A fast
model that is not trusted creates more risk than value.
Budget owner: VP/Director R&D, simulation engineering, computational science, digital
engineering, data science, technical product; GPU/platform budgets with infrastructure, the
business case with engineering/operations.
Procurement: narrowly defined use case with measurable baseline (one solver, one geometry
family, one decision loop, one validation protocol). Vendors sell platform access; a solo
specialist complements with independent problem framing, validation, adoption design.
Entry offer: Scientific-Computing Validation Diligence - $7.5k-$10k target. Deliver: use-case
selection; data and simulator audit; baseline-versus-surrogate evaluation plan; error and
uncertainty framework; reproducibility checklist; deployment risks; go/no-go recommendation.
Expansion: $15k briefing/training customized around the customer's solver, data, and workflow
- never generic AI education.
Why EVEZ fits: connect simulation outputs to agent orchestration and provenance. Strongest
proof: a reproducible demonstration logging inputs, model/version, conditions, outputs,
confidence, validation results. A directory name alone is not proof of scientific validity.

## 3. SECONDARY CHANNELS

### QUANTUM COMPUTING COMPANIES
IBM's public segmentation (free exploration / pay-as-you-go / project capacity / premium
annual / on-prem) implies distinct buyer motions [5]. EVEZ sells quantum use-case and
evidence diligence, not hardware competition or generic strategy: a $5k-$10k assessment of
whether a workload has a credible quantum-advantage hypothesis, the classical baseline
required, the data/error assumptions that matter, the next experiment. Buyers: quantum
startup enterprise-innovation teams, corporate quantum programs, funds evaluating quantum
companies.

### DEEP-TECH VC FUNDS AND CORPORATE VENTURE TEAMS
Funds invest across quantum, foundational AI, robotics, autonomy [8]. The pain: decision
compression - is the claim novel, buildable, defensible, scalable, connected to a buyer?
Product: fixed-scope technical diligence memo with explicit evidence ledger, claim
decomposition, architecture review, competitor/alternative baseline, key experiments, red
flags. Buyer: partner, principal, platform lead, scout - not the portfolio engineering team.
Route: one or two pilot memos at fixed fee, then named expert for recurring technical review.
Do not claim funds routinely pay a particular rate - not established.

FALSIFIER: this doctrine fails if any client-facing material claims certification ability,
quantum-safety, production hardening, or market-verified rates contrary to the Unsupported
list above.
